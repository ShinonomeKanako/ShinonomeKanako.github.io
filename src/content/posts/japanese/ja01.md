## Simple Question and Answer
> サントスさんは学生じゃありません。

`じゃありません` represents NOT, and its formal case is `ではありません`。

> ミラーさんは会社員ですか。
あなたは誰ですか？

In formal Japanese, we don't use `?`, but young speakers may use `?` after question sentences. `ですか` ends with a **rising** tone.

> ミラーさんは会社員です。サントスさん**も**会社員です。

`も` represents *also*, the same with the one that has been mentioned before.

｀じゃありません｀ is the polite form of `じゃない`

> これ　それ　あれ
この　その　あの

これ refer to things within the reach of the speaker, それ within the reach of the person you're speaking to, and あれ out of reach of you both.

> これは何ですか？
それは雑誌です。

> わたしの傘 
日本語の本
カメラの雑誌 

## `の` の使いの方

`の` represents belonging or content. However, the question form is different, as follows:

> これは誰の傘ですか？
それは何の本ですか？

> この本はいくらですか？
How much is this book?

## Selective Question

> それはボールペンですか？シャープペンシルですか？
Is this a ballpoint pen(ballpen) or mechanical pencil(sharp pencil)?
バールペンです。
This is a ballpoint pen.

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ

`ここ`、`そこ`、`あそこ`、`どこ` are used to point to places.

> ここは食堂です。

`ここ` means "here", and `食堂` means dining hall or cafeteria. The sentence means:

> This place is the cafeteria.

| ことば | 意味 | 使い方 |
|---|---|---|
| ここ | here | The place close to the speaker. |
| そこ | there | The place close to the listener, or the place just mentioned. |
| あそこ | over there | The place far from both the speaker and the listener. |
| どこ | where | Used to ask about a place. |

:::note
The key is the speaker's point of view. `ここ` is the speaker's area, `そこ` is the listener's area, and `あそこ` is away from both sides. When asking for the location, use `どこ`. When two speakers stand together and the location is not far away, use `そこ` instead of `あそこ`.
:::

`どこの` 可能是问产地，也可能是问牌子。

> このネクタイはいくらですか？
